## Rules
- Beats must cover the full script in time order. Each beat has start and end_seconds (cumulative timeline).
- Every beat (aroll or broll) must be aligned to the **exact lines being spoken** in that time range, not generic ideas from elsewhere in the script.
- type "aroll" = show the speaker (recorded video). Max 3-5 seconds before cutting to broll or a zoom cut. Follow the B-Roll Ratio and Editing Pacing rules from the Reference Material.
- type "broll" = show an AI-generated clip instead of the speaker. Use for: hook (first 2-3s), mechanism reveals, proof shots, visual interrupts. Every broll beat MUST have "suggestion" using this exact format: "[Subject performing a physical action]. Camera: [one clear camera move]. [Scene/lighting/environment detail]."
- B-roll suggestions must be **literal, concrete visuals** that clearly depict what the viewer just heard or is hearing. Prefer specific locations and **observable body language**. Obey the Brand & scene block below for casting, setting, and what to avoid.
- Ban emotion adjectives in suggestions (fear, apprehension, hope, tension, obedience). Show them as actions: frozen hands in lap, eyes darting aside, quick nod while looking down, shoulders tightening.
- Each suggestion MUST match the **exact spoken lines** in that beat's time range (not a generic theme from elsewhere).
- Keep people casting consistent across B-roll beats when people appear (same look / wardrobe / age band as brand casting).
- Metaphorical or abstract shots (forests, rivers, glowing walls, generic light patterns) are allowed at most **once per script** and only when they obviously reinforce an explicitly spoken symbol.
- Hook: The FIRST beat MUST be aroll (speaker face-to-camera hook, 3-5 seconds). The speaker's face grabs attention. Follow with broll after.
- Filler words (um, uh) will be detected later and covered with a neutral B-roll clip + muted audio automatically.
- Aim for at least 40% broll coverage to keep the video visually dynamic.
- Prefer each b-roll beat about **3–5 seconds** long (pipeline caps display at ~5s per B-roll clip).

## B-roll Examples (Bad vs Good)
- Bad (emotion adjective, not filmable): "A person with visible apprehension at a table. Camera: slow push-in."
- Bad (too abstract for a line about copying tension): "Water gently flowing around stones in a forest stream. Camera: slow pan. Golden hour light."
- Good (literal body language): "A child sitting very still at a kitchen island, hands flat on thighs, eyes tracking a parent off-frame. Camera: tight push-in from doorway. Warm daylight."
- Good (literal for quiet compliance): "Someone nodding quickly while looking down at their hands as another person speaks nearby. Camera: over-shoulder handheld. Soft lamp light in a lived-in room."
